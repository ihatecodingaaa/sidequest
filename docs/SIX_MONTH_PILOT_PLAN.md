# Six month pilot plan

What SIDEQUEST would actually do in six months, with a small team and no
budget assumptions that have not been checked.

Dates and the participant target match the submitted Delta Challenge 2026
application and `docs/submission/SIDEQUEST_Content_and_Experience_Synopsis.pdf`.
Where this plan and the filing disagree, the filing is what was submitted and
this document is wrong.

Deliberately unambitious about reach and deliberately specific about content,
because the product's weakness is not that it needs more users. It is that no
young person in Singapore has written a word of the content yet.

---

## The honest starting position

| | |
| - | - |
| Participants to date | **Zero** |
| Data collected | **None** |
| Organisations engaged | **None** |
| Content authored by young people | **None** |
| Deployment | Not deployed |

Everything below starts from there. A plan that opens with "scale nationally"
from that position is not a plan.

---

## Month 1, November 2026: co-design, and finding out what is wrong

**The goal is to break the content, not to validate it.**

- Two sessions with 8 to 12 young people in the 13 to 19 band, run with a
  school or a community partner.
- Play the two threads. Watch where people stop reading, where they guess, and
  where the language sounds like an adult wrote it. It did.
- **Test the four Signal modes as vocabulary.** The specific question: after
  ten minutes, can somebody say what Redirect means without looking? If four
  modes turn out to be one too many, the finding is worth more than the design.
- Collect scenarios. The Build a Quest board exists to be filled in this month.

**Deliverable:** a list of what is wrong, and at least six youth-written
scenario drafts.

**Risk:** no partner agrees to host a session. Mitigation: run it as an
informal group rather than through an institution, and say so in the write-up.

---

## Month 2, November to December 2026: rewrite, and prepare the people who will run it

- Rewrite the shipped content against Month 1. Expect to lose material.
- Turn the best three youth drafts into playable threads, credited to their
  authors, with the authors reviewing the result.
- Build the facilitator pack: a 40 minute session plan, what to say, what to do
  when the wifi fails, and the pre and post instruments from
  `docs/PILOT_MEASUREMENT_PLAN.md`.
- **Fix the review pipeline properly.** Drafts are currently local and go
  nowhere. A pilot needs a real path from draft to reviewed to published, even
  if that path is a facilitator with a laptop.

**Deliverable:** version 2 content, a facilitator pack, three youth-authored
threads.

---

## Month 3, December 2026: dry run, then Pilot Wave 1

- A dry run with staff first, then Pilot Wave 1 with one or two school or
  community groups. 50 to 60 participants.
- Two sessions a fortnight apart, because multiple sessions is one of the three
  moderators in the serious games literature and a single session tests the
  wrong thing.
- Crew Shift run properly, with actual groups of three or four, because that is
  the mechanic's whole point and the Solo Preview is not a substitute for it.
- Pre instrument at session one, post at session two.

**Deliverable:** the first real data this project has ever had.

**Risk:** the sessions go badly. That is an acceptable outcome in Month 3 and an
expensive one in Month 5, which is why it is here.

---

## Month 4, January 2027: iterate, and build the second pack

- Fix what session feedback exposed.
- Build the **secondary-band pack**: the same mechanisms, written for a younger
  audience. The architecture supports it now; this month proves it by shipping
  one rather than by asserting it.
- Approach one retailer about the shop floor content. Not for sponsorship: for
  accuracy. A shop manager will find things wrong with the scenario that no
  amount of desk research would.

**Deliverable:** a second content pack, and one review from somebody who
actually works in retail.

---

## Month 5, February 2027: Pilot Wave 2

- Pilot Wave 2: two more groups, ideally one secondary and one
  post-secondary, so the age adaptation is tested rather than described. 60 to
  70 participants.
- Same instruments, so the numbers are comparable.
- Total across the pilot: approximately **150** secondary and post-secondary
  youths, which is the target in the submitted application. Wave 1, Wave 2 and
  the March activation together. Enough for a description. **Not** enough for a
  claim about effect.

**Deliverable:** comparable data from three cohorts across two age bands.

---

## Month 6, March to April 2027: activation, evaluation and handover

- **March 2027: activation.** A roadshow or community session that reaches
  the remainder of the cohort and puts the content in front of people who did
  not sit a full pilot session.
- **April 2027: evaluation.** Analyse against the plan, including the measures
  that did not move.
- Write the limitations section first, so the rest is written under it.
- Hand over: content pack format, facilitator materials, the youth authoring
  route, and a documented list of everything that broke.
- Decide honestly whether this should continue. A pilot that cannot conclude
  "stop" was never an evaluation.

**Deliverable:** an evaluation that a reader can disagree with, because the
numbers and the limitations are both in it.

---

## What this plan does not include, and why

| Not included | Why |
| ------------ | --- |
| A control group | Not feasible at this scale with this recruitment route. It is the main reason the evaluation will be descriptive, and that is stated rather than hidden. |
| National deployment | Three cohorts is not a basis for it. |
| A backend | Nothing in six months needs one. Local state is sufficient and its limits are known. |
| An SPF or NCPC partnership | It would be the single most valuable thing to have and it is not ours to schedule. The product stays honest without it: both remain sources and potential partners, never partners. |
| A crime outcome measure | See `docs/PILOT_MEASUREMENT_PLAN.md`. |

---

## The three things most likely to go wrong

1. **No institution says yes,** and the whole plan slips a month at Month 1.
   Everything downstream compresses, and the honest response is to cut Month 5
   rather than to cut Month 3.
2. **The four-mode vocabulary does not land.** Recoverable, and better found in
   Month 1 than in a submission. Three modes would be a real simplification.
3. **Crew Shift does not get run with real groups,** because it is the hardest
   thing to schedule and the easiest to skip. If it is skipped, the pilot has
   tested the solo product and should say so rather than reporting on group
   play it did not observe.
